Why moths fly towards light

For centuries people have watched moths circle lamps, candles and street lights, and wondered why they do it.

PlaceObservation
Street lampCircling moths
Dark clearingSteady flight

New work with high-speed cameras suggests the insects are not attracted to light at all, but keep their backs turned towards it, as they would to the bright sky.

Near a lamp, that rule makes them tilt, turn and stall, so they end up trapped in orbits around it, the researchers say.

The finding could help design lighting that leaves insects alone, which matters for the many birds and bats that eat them at night.
